01 · Principles and postureOne page · read before anything else
An instrument, not a coach, in a reading room.

Applied Research finds papers, maps a domain by prerequisite depth, and puts the paper on the whole screen. The AI builds scaffolding — sources, explanations, argument maps. The interface exists to provoke and capture the user's reactions. The AI may author facts. Only the human authors insights and theses, and the design must show that without a single label.

Never speaks firstNo nudges. No "you haven't reacted in a while." One exemption: acknowledgement. Save settles (220ms) and offers Undo for 5s as a text button; no toast.
Renders state, continuouslyHow much you've reacted to, how deep you are, what you know versus don't. The seeing is the provocation.
The paper is the screenWhen reading, chrome recedes. Column max 68ch, centered.
Affordance at point of contactA text selection, a dark region on the map — and nowhere else. Shortcut hints appear inside the toolbar on hover ("Explain ⌘K"), not only in tooltips.
Provenance is structural, not a badge
Why divide by √dk?AI-authored text sits on a surface: inset container one tonal step above the ground, hairline left rule, neutral ink, interface face. Surface holds what the AI asserts; ground holds what the human asserts; a question belongs to whichever it heads — so the human's question sits inside the card, in the reading face, italic.
Human-authored text sits on the ground, outdented 28px into the left margin behind a 3px human-rule. Reading face, human-ink. Reads as margin handwriting.
The paper is the paper: the reading face on the ground, no wash until the user reacts to a passage.
If a component needs a "YOU" or "AI" label to be understood, it is wrong.
Two hues, two meanings, never on the same pixel
Warm means what the human made: the reacted passage, the human margin rule, the lamp on the map, the Brief's wash. Cold means what the interface offers: hover, active, the citation under the cursor, the frontier line on the map, Add and Cite while touched. Rest states are achromatic. Pen and wash: on light, pastel is an area, never a line or a letter — a line that must carry hue uses the loaded form of the same pigment (sepia). On dark, warm appears only as light; no yellow ink on basalt. Chromatic ink on dark is the cold.

Attention is all you need · §3.2.1dark

We call our particular attention "Scaled Dot-Product Attention". The input consists of queries and keys of dimension dk, and values of dimension dv. We compute the dot products of the query with all keys, divide each by √dk, and apply a softmax function to obtain the weights on the values.

Why divide by √dk and not dk?
If q and k have unit-variance components, their dot product has variance dk, so its standard deviation is √dk. Dividing by √dk restores unit variance; dividing by dk would collapse the logits toward zero and flatten the softmax. §3.2.1
Ask follow-upNote in my words
So the scale is a variance fix, not a magic constant. For my retrieval layer the analogue is normalizing embedding dot products before ranking — I've been skipping this.
The two most commonly used attention functions are additive attention, and dot-product (multiplicative) attention.

3.2 Attention

An attention function can be described as mapping a query and a set of key-value pairs to an output, where the query, keys, values, and output are all vectors. The output is computed as a weighted sum of the values, where the weight assigned to each value is computed by a compatibility function of the query with the corresponding key.

We call our particular attention "Scaled Dot-Product Attention". The input consists of queries and keys of dimension dk, and values of dimension dv. We compute the dot products of the query with all keys, divide each by √dk, and apply a softmax function to obtain the weights on the values.

3.2 Attention

An attention function can be described as mapping a query and a set of key-value pairs to an output, where the query, keys, values, and output are all vectors. The output is computed as a weighted sum of the values, where the weight assigned to each value is computed by a compatibility function of the query with the corresponding key.
