## Problem Statement

Builders learning an unfamiliar domain to build in it lose ~10 hours/week to the logistics of research: finding sources, going down rabbit holes when they hit unfamiliar concepts mid-read, and context switching between tools (search → PDF → chat → notes → citation manager). Existing tools each solve a slice and none are built around the actual goal: the builder developing an owned, defensible point of view (a "brainlift": DOK-tiered knowledge artifact where AI may gather facts but the human must author insights and spiky POVs) — and, increasingly, a distilled context artifact their coding agents can build from.

**Core thesis (the contrarian bet):** every competitor races to do more of the thinking for the user (better reports, better summaries — "construction"). The value of research is in the *reaction*, not the construction. Applied Research is a reaction engine: AI constructs the scaffolding (sources, explanations, argument graphs — DOK1), and the interface is engineered to provoke and capture the user's reactions, which compile into the brainlift and the agent-context pack.

## Demand Evidence

- Founder dogfooding: "I spend and easily lose 10 hours a week from the logistical friction required in brainlifts/research — just finding different sources, going down rabbit holes when I don't understand something or need an explanation, and context switching."
- Beachhead population: GauntletAI cohort (AI engineers) are **required** to produce brainlifts on a cadence — forced, recurring production of exactly the artifact this tool outputs.
- Cohort-mates "said they would use such a tool" — flagged in-session as interest, not demand. No named user yet; no observed usage yet. This is the biggest evidence gap (see The Assignment).
- Tool-failure specifics (behavioral): ChatGPT/Claude deep research → noisy reports, no learning interface. Elicit → finds/screens papers well but abandons the user at "I don't understand this concept." NotebookLM → learning-ish but no in-tool source discovery, forces context-switch to chat instead of annotating the paper, sloppy generated outputs.

## Status Quo

Duct-tape workflow: Elicit (find papers) + NotebookLM/chat tools (understand them) + manual note assembly into brainlift docs + manual packaging of context for coding agents. ~10 hrs/week of logistics for the founder; the same friction reported anecdotally across the cohort.

### DOK legend (Webb's Depth of Knowledge, as used by brainlifts)

- **DOK1** — recall/raw facts: source summaries, findings, citations. *AI may author.*
- **DOK2** — concepts in your own words (knowledge tree). *Human-authored; AI may draft candidates the human must rewrite.*
- **DOK3** — strategic thinking: insights (decision rules) and blueprints (workflows). *Human-only.*
- **DOK4** — extended thinking: spiky POVs (contrarian, falsifiable claims). *Human-only.*

Event-type → DOK mapping in the reaction schema: `highlight/question/answer/summary` → DOK1; `note` (concept restatement) → DOK2; `insight` → DOK3; `spov` → DOK4; `agree/dispute/unsure` are reactions attached to DOK1 objects that can be promoted into DOK3/4 drafts. Allowed values of `DOK level`: 1–4. Records with DOK ≥ 3 and `author: AI` are unrepresentable in the schema.

## Premises (all six agreed)

1. Target user is a **builder** learning an unfamiliar domain to build in it — not an academic doing systematic lit review. Concede Elicit's market; compete on speed-to-defensible-understanding.
2. The bottleneck is the **mid-read learning loop**, not search or summarization. Defended against cross-model challenge with mechanism (transfer is the hard part of learning; context switching disrupts transfer; source selection is already easy with existing tools) — but still unobserved beyond the founder. Treated as the primary hypothesis; the Reader doubles as the experiment. (Note: in-tool discovery is justified by killing the tab-switch out of the reading context, not by beating Elicit at search — see Recommended Approach.)
3. Brainlift doctrine as **architecture**: AI constructs DOK1; human authors DOK3/DOK4; the UI provokes and captures reactions. Enforced in the data schema (DOK3/4 records cannot carry `author: AI`).
4. **Agent-ready context is a first-class output** — a research session ends in an artifact a coding agent builds from. Unclaimed territory across Elicit/NotebookLM/scite/Anara.
5. The **argument graph is a means** (credibility x-ray + reaction surface), not the product. Rendered as a compact panel anchored to the paper — never a standalone canvas (argument mapping's 200-year failure: construction cost + text↔diagram switching cost, both of which LLM generation + in-document anchoring remove).
6. **Gauntlet cohort beachhead, open-source builders expansion.**

- **B. The Full Loop (chosen):** discovery + reading workspace + paper-anchored argument views + DOK-tiered brainlift vault + agent-context compiler, open source. Scope note: B is the *product* scope; the *capstone* deliverable (Sept 9) is milestone A + whichever gated layers survive the 9-day clock (see Build gates) — the rest continues post-capstone as open source.

## Recommended Approach

**B — the Full Loop — executed as parallel teammate tracks on top of C's reaction-event data model, with A as the first integrated milestone.**

- **Data model (the contract between tracks):** every interaction is a reaction event with provenance: `{source span, event type (highlight | question | answer | summary | agree | dispute | unsure | note | insight | spov), DOK level, author: human|AI, timestamps, refs?}`. `refs` is an optional list of links to other events or source spans — **including spans in other papers** — with its own author tag, so both humans and AI can cross-link annotations across sources (e.g. a dispute on paper A's claim citing paper B's finding as the rebuttal; a DOK2 note grounded in three papers). The argument panel's cross-source rebuttals and the compiled exports' citation trails are views over `refs`. The brainlift document, argument views, and agent-context pack are compiled views over this log. DOK3/DOK4 events with `author: AI` are unrepresentable. Storage is git-friendly (markdown/JSON files; Obsidian-compatible vault) so coding agents read it natively.
- **Parallel tracks:** Track 1 — reading workspace: PDF rendering, highlight→ask anchored threads with citations, reaction capture, dispute→POV drafting. Track 2 — compiled exports (`brainlift.md` + `agent-context.md`) + argument distillation (Toulmin-style claims/grounds/warrants/rebuttals with evidence-strength scoring) + in-tool discovery (scholarly APIs, e.g. Semantic Scholar/arXiv/OpenAlex). Integration contract: the reaction-event schema, pinned on day one. **Ownership: assign one named owner per track (Aaryan + teammate); TBD here, to be resolved by EOD Sept 1 and recorded in the repo copy.** (Day numbering throughout: day 1 = Monday Sept 1; Demo Day = day 9.) Milestone A is cross-track by design: it needs Track 1's reading surface AND Track 2's export compiler — that dependency is the critical path, so the export compiler is Track 2's first deliverable, before distillation or discovery — which requires a **v0 `agent-context.md` schema pinned by the Track 2 owner before milestone A** (Open Question 5 then governs the v1 refinement after the day 7–8 eval).
- **First integrated milestone (milestone A) = Approach A's surface:** one paper, read end-to-end in-tool, exporting `brainlift.md` + `agent-context.md`. This is also the premise-2 experiment: measure context switches, completion time, quality of human-authored POVs on real cohort assignments. **Given Demo Day is Sept 9, milestone A must land within ~4 days so the remaining days go to cohort validation sessions and the demo-differentiating layer.**
- **Build gates (explicit, per review):** argument distillation and in-tool discovery are sequenced AFTER milestone A and shaped by the Assignment's observation outcome. Rationale for keeping them in capstone scope at all: the product-track requirement "not trivial to copy in a weekend" is answered by the distillation + enforcement schema, and discovery's justification is killing the tab-switch out of the reading context (not out-searching Elicit). If the 9-day clock forces a cut, discovery is cut before distillation, and distillation ships as the compact per-paper argument panel only.

## Design pass (2026-09-02) — settled

Grilled end-to-end with the founder; supersedes the SETTLED items above where they conflict. Full prompts: `prompt-1-design-system.md` (system) and `prompt-2-screens.md` (screens), to be run in that order in Claude Design.

**Product**
- Posture: instrument in a reading room. Renders state, never speaks first. Provocation comes from seeing your own reaction pattern, not from nudges.
- Goal object: the **Brief** ("I'm building X and need to understand Y"), project-scoped. Open questions are the mechanism; the DOK ladder is a readout, never a per-paper meter.
- Two entries, one home: *start from a question* (runs a ≤2-minute calibration) or *start from a paper* (no interview). Both land on the **Map**.
- Map: concepts as horizontal layers by prerequisite depth, foundations at the bottom. Known territory is a watercolor wash that fades at the user's edge. Fixed layout, no dragging, changes only through reactions. Papers are marks on the layers.
- Calibration ("What do you already know about [domain]?"): AI proposes the Map; user marks only the seam layers Know it / Heard of it / No idea; "Heard of it" asks for a one-sentence explanation, which becomes the first Note. Marking is a `known` event.
- Reader: paper **reflowed** into the product's typography (not PDF + overlay); original PDF one click away as fallback. Inline cards for answers; **rabbit-hole layers** for sources. Rule: a card is an answer, a layer is a source; a card never exceeds the viewport. Depth strip shows the path ("2 DEEP"). Esc returns to the exact line.
- Shell: left = where (projects, sources by depth, density marks; collapsible; not a file tree). Right = what (**Arguments · Questions · Annotations**). Map and reader are peers in the center; the map becomes a strip while reading.
- Reactions, from first principles: agree/dispute deleted as primitives (they are verdicts a reader at their edge can't issue). Toolbar is **Explain · Note · Counter · Idea**. No Highlight action (marking without saying something is not a reaction; the wash is earned by a reaction). Counter resolves to *I was missing something* / *The paper has a problem* / *Still open*; the second spawns a Critique thesis. Founder chose "Counter" over "Push back" knowing it reads slightly as a verdict; resolution copy compensates.
- Workbench, third peer state: **Inbox** (all reactions) → **Thinking** (insights and theses, human-only writing) → **Playbook** (compiled output + **Context pack**). AI verbs in the Workbench: *show* (suggestions tray beside the field, never in it; Critique only) and **stress-test** (strongest cited case against a thesis, from the user's own papers only; never checks the industry half of a Gap).
- **Thesis** replaces "spiky POV". Two kinds: **Critique** (a specific paper's claim is wrong/no longer holds) and **Gap** (research established it, industry hasn't absorbed it). Shared spine: claim · evidence (≥1 ref, required) · what would prove me wrong. Kind field is free text, no fault taxonomy. Optional: why this hasn't been noticed.
- Discovery: never a search page. Entry points are a dark layer's **Find papers for this** and links inside answers. `⌘K` is a finder, not discovery. Sources are papers only in v1; industry sources cut (existing tools do that well). The Gap thesis's "what the industry does instead" field is human-written from the user's own knowledge.
- Export is the Workbench's Playbook state, not a rail button.
- Ladder shown as **Facts · Notes · Insights · Theses**; DOK numbers never appear in the UI. "Brainlift" is not used in the product; the artifact is the Playbook.

**Schema deltas**
- Event types: `question | answer | summary | note | counter | resolution(gap|problem|open) | idea | insight | thesis(critique|gap) | known`. `highlight`, `agree`, `dispute`, `unsure`, `spov` removed.
- `refs` unchanged. DOK ≥ 3 with `author: AI` remains unrepresentable.

**Visual system**
- Provenance by material, not badges: AI text on a surface (one tonal step up, hairline left rule); human text on the ground with a pastel wash; the paper on the ground, washed only where reacted to.
- Type: Newsreader (paper + human writing), Familjen Grotesk (UI + AI), Martian Mono uppercase (locators, chips, strip, labels), Fraunces for exactly two display moments (project title, Brief).
- Color: dark default basalt (`#0C0F12` / surface `#14181C`), light warm white (`#FBFAF7` / `#F3F1EC`), warm-gray ink ramps. One accent meaning "a human was here": a warm pastel run (straw → cream → pale rose, provisional), straw as the flat accent for marks/washes, the run as a watercolor wash for the Map's known territory and covers (one per screen, never <200px, never inside the paper). Exclusions: violet/indigo, blue, orange/coral/vermillion, any status hue. Accent run to be tuned; foundation approved.
- Motion: consequence-only, ease-out 150–200ms, no springs/loops, paper never reflows above the reader's eye, reduced motion cuts to end state. Three signatures: card open, layer push, wash spread (the only slow one).
- References the founder responded to: rachelchen.tech, johnyvino.com, amylalai.com (triad type, one accent used once, uppercase mono meta register, one bold gesture per screen).
