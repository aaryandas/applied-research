# University course and open textbook expansion plan

Research checkpoint: 9 September 2026. Read-only; no ingestion, provider calls, purchases or outreach performed. This is a product/engineering permission assessment, not a legal determination. It supplements the approved turbopuffer/Qwen configuration without increasing the $0.25 initial embedding allowance.

## Recommendation

Deliver university-source coverage through two working paths: a searchable directory that opens original course resources, and exact permission-verified text assets that support canonical reading, vector retrieval and generated lessons. MIT is not permanently blocked: its independently published Computational Thinking repository explicitly licenses text under CC BY-SA 4.0, unlike the general OCW noncommercial license. Start with BCcampus and TU Delft CC BY chapters, then add this MIT material with ShareAlike handling and passive notebook extraction.

## MIT OCW, Yale and CMU remain part of the learning experience

### Course discovery and external reading now

Store a bounded, operator-reviewed directory of minimal factual fields: original URL, institution, course code/title, instructor, term, level, broad subject tags, resource types, last checked date and license/permission status. Display original-source attribution and an **Open course** action. Keep the user's bookmarks, notes, progress and reflections locally associated with that URL. Do not copy original marketing descriptions, entire syllabi, transcripts or screenshots into the commercial catalog without applicable permission. No CC0 MIT/Yale/CMU catalog license was verified here; a comprehensive automated bulk catalog is a separate permission/access review, not implied by public availability.

Concrete initial course entries:

- [MIT 6.100L, Introduction to CS and Programming using Python, Fall 2022](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-100l-introduction-to-cs-and-programming-using-python-fall-2022/), Ana Bell. Official page links notes, lecture code, videos and programming exercises. Link to the original course and resource pages; do not mark them acquired/indexed merely because discoverable.
- [Yale PHYS 200, Fundamentals of Physics I](https://oyc.yale.edu/physics/phys-200), Ramamurti Shankar, Fall 2006. Official course and session links offer a coherent external physics course. Its recommended commercial textbooks are independent references, not licensed course assets.
- [CMU OLI Introduction to Psychology — Open & Free](https://oli.cmu.edu/courses/introduction-to-psychology-open-free/). Open the supported course experience; do not scrape authenticated course sessions or pretend a public landing page includes all interactive learning material.

After external study, learners can record their own reflection and practical results. The app may use separately permitted evidence for tutor explanations while identifying its actual sources; it must not claim an answer is based on the unacquired MIT/Yale/CMU course. Linking an original course into a learner's plan is useful coverage, even while full-text indexing permissions remain narrower.

### Exact restrictions and routes to broader use

MIT's current general OCW license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. MIT's terms give paid commercial courses based on OCW materials as a prohibited example, and separately restrict AI training to noncommercial use. RAG is not identical to model training; that distinction alone does not erase the copying, commercial-purpose or adaptation questions. The course's official downloads provide a technical offline acquisition route, but downloading availability is not new commercial permission. Review exact assets or obtain scoped permission for managed commercial RAG. [MIT terms, updated 11 August 2026](https://ocw.mit.edu/pages/privacy-and-terms-of-use/), [official course download example](https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s191-introduction-to-computational-thinking-fall-2022/download/)

Yale's terms specify BY-NC-SA attribution and expressly identify paid tutor/premium services and advertising as prohibited commercial reuse examples. Third-party lecture credits need separate examination. Start with external links; request permission for commercial excerpt display, indexing and generation rather than assuming a free plan inside a paid product solves the issue. [Open Yale Courses terms](https://oyc.yale.edu/terms)

CMU's open-and-free learner access is a service-access offering, not a blanket transfer of all course rights. Its Torus **software** is MIT-licensed and welcomes commercial use, while a checked [Media Programming course](https://oli.cmu.edu/courses/media-programming-copy/) uses CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Verify the license on each chosen course/export or arrange permission with CMU; never substitute the platform software license for the content license. [OLI terms](https://oli.cmu.edu/about/policies/oli-terms-and-conditions/), [Torus commercial-use statement](https://oli.cmu.edu/faq-items/can-a-business-use-the-platform/)

## Can a private, noncommercial learner opt into MIT OCW RAG?

**A narrower personal-use mode is worth evaluating, but a checkbox plus account isolation is not sufficient evidence of permission for this managed commercial service.** Creative Commons says NC depends on the actual use and purpose, not simply whether the user/provider is nonprofit or for-profit. Copyright exceptions and the classification of adaptations depend on jurisdiction and facts. We should not promise that private embeddings or RAG outputs are automatically outside those rights. [CC FAQ: noncommercial use](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#does-my-use-violate-the-noncommercial-clause-of-the-licenses)

A personal, genuinely noncommercial learner copying selected licensed material for their own study is a different proposed use from the company building a reusable course corpus or selling source-derived tutoring. If explored, document the exact design for rights-holder/counsel review:

1. Learner selects a specific course/asset and purpose; never authorize an entire NC provider or institution by account type alone.
2. Source bytes, canonical text, embeddings, source-derived explanations and caches remain confined to that learner. No shared retrieval, public course publishing, content resale, shared model training or company-wide reuse.
3. Preserve license, creator, source location, acquisition timestamp, asset exceptions and transformations. Source-derived exports must carry applicable attribution/SA/NC restrictions where required; opting out deletes derived vectors/caches as well as text.
4. Identify every processor, including company backend, OpenRouter route and turbopuffer, plus retained data, billing and training-use terms. A paid platform's processing for the learner remains an unresolved use-purpose question even if storage is technically isolated.
5. Obtain written confirmation or a legal assessment of that actual managed-service use before labeling it permitted. An entirely learner-controlled offline workflow could change the analysis, but adding local inference or user-managed keys would be a new product/architecture decision, not an implementation shortcut.

Until resolved, deliver the original-site reader/link workflow and independently permitted MIT assets. This keeps MIT in scope today and provides a concrete path for broader managed ingestion rather than an indefinite generic block.

Suggested permission inquiry (prepare only; no message sent): identify selected course URLs and ask whether a paid learning application may (a) retrieve/cache text for individual users, (b) create embeddings through named no-training processors, (c) show attributed excerpts, (d) generate private source-linked explanations, and (e) export or share adapted learning material. Ask whether per-learner isolation/noncommercial use changes the answer and what attribution, deletion and licensing obligations apply. Seek separate clarity on third-party course assets.

## Backend delivery slices and acceptance

**First slice: verified HTML corpus.** Add a handful of BCcampus and TU Delft chapters with explicit manifest scope, source/license hashes and immutable canonical versions. Use the approved real turbopuffer pipeline and fixed Qwen configuration. Preserve section/paragraph locators, equations, tables and code; do not promote extracted content to complete when material is omitted. The current `canonicalize.ts` explicitly excludes MathML, images, SVG, video and iframes, marks those omissions partial, and accepts only HTML/plain text. TU Delft's available Markdown is a clear next format opportunity. Acquisition permission and extraction quality are independent gates.

**Second slice: licensed MIT notebooks.** Freeze the official Fall24 repository SHA, inspect each selected notebook's third-party asset notices, passively extract supported Markdown/code cells into separately attributed canonical blocks, and preserve source/cell location plus text-vs-code license. Do not evaluate notebooks or install Julia. Introduce attribution/export handling for source-derived shared material before allowing ShareAlike text into generated shareable lessons. Non-text/media-dependent sections remain coverage gaps with original-site links.

**Third slice: university directory and permission workflow.** Add the small factual course directory, resource links and explicit states (`external-reading`, `permitted-content`, `format-pending`, `permission-pending`, `indexed`). Extend discovery through the [Open Textbook Library's CC0 metadata and JSON API](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/discovery), then resolve content rights at each publisher. Do not imply catalog metadata is lesson evidence. Add source-specific permission records so future MIT/Yale/CMU permissions can be activated without replacing the architecture.

Acceptance for every indexed source: real permission record, canonical byte/text hashes and citation locator; exactly scoped backend ownership; real vector write/query; query hit resolves to exact stored passage; unsupported/omitted content produces a truthful gap; cross-account access is denied; duplicate ingestion does not re-embed; attribution survives the Reader and export. The initial evaluation shares the existing total $0.25 ceiling and must stop before exhausting it. This plan does not authorize larger ingestion spending.
