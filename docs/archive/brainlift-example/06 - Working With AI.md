*The hard rule, in full — and how to use AI tooling without breaking it.*

## The hard rule

**BrainLifts are not AI-generated.** This is the single non-negotiable rule of the whole practice.

Specifically: the **synthesis tiers must pass through your own brain**. AI may gather raw facts and find experts. It must never write the thinking.

## Why (this is the whole point)

The purpose of a BrainLift is to build *your* expertise and *your* defensible point of view. AI-generating it defeats that purpose and yields average, un-owned content that sounds fine and means nothing.

> The value is in the reaction, not the construction.

Editing text an AI wrote is **not** the same as thinking it through. You can polish a generated SPOV all day and still not own it — because you never did the reasoning that produced it. The knowledge has to be built, not received. That's why a long, polished BrainLift produced in one sitting is the exact red flag this rule exists to catch.

## The green / red line

### AI is allowed to (green):
- **Scaffold** the blank note — all the section headers, empty.
- Gather **DOK1** — key facts, objective source summaries (what the source *says*), per-source limitations, and relevance. This is factual extraction.
- Assemble the **Reference list**.
- Propose the **Experts** candidate list.
- Propose a *candidate* **Title / Purpose / In-scope / Out-of-scope / Owner** — clearly marked as drafts for you to accept or rewrite. (This is framing, not DOK synthesis.)
- Seed **"questions to chase"** — questions only, never positions.
- **Audit / critique** any tier: find gaps, pressure-test, point out soft SPOVs — output findings and questions, never replacement prose.

### AI must NEVER (red):
- Write or draft the **DOK2 Knowledge Tree** (this counts as synthesis — it's yours).
- Write or draft **DOK3 Insights**.
- Write or draft **DOK3 Blueprints**.
- Write or draft **DOK4 Spiky Points of View**.
- "Rough in a provisional version for you to edit." Editing AI synthesis is not thinking it through.

A simple way to remember it: **AI handles the facts (DOK1) and the packaging; you handle the meaning (DOK2, DOK3, DOK4).**

## The tools that support this

Two Claude Code skills exist to run the practice while staying hard-bounded below DOK3. You don't need them to do BrainLifts — a text editor is enough — but this is how the workflow is automated in-house.

**`brainlift-today`** — the *daily input* end. It creates today's blank dated note, reviews what you've already covered so suggestions don't repeat, proposes bounded topics with a "spiky question" to chase, and — once you pick a topic — gathers a DOK1 fact sheet and expert list into the note. It leaves everything below the line blank for you. Run it once a day.

**`brainlift-promote`** — the *output* end. Two modes: (A) scaffold a full Anatomy-structured BrainLift from your accumulated dailies, filling only the green layers (DOK1, experts, references, candidate framing) and leaving the human-only tiers blank with a "YOURS — do not AI-generate" marker; and (B) *audit* an existing BrainLift — critique scope, evidence, and SPOV strength in priority order, without ever rewriting your synthesis.

Both tools are deliberately built as capture-and-structure aids with AI hard-bounded below DOK3 — never content generators.

## How to prompt AI safely

Good prompts (green):
- "Gather a balanced, sourced DOK1 fact sheet on [topic] — ~5 best sources, real numbers, and be honest about null or contested findings."
- "Find the 5–8 experts whose work underpins [topic] and where to follow them."
- "Here's my draft SPOV. **Audit it**: is it actually contrarian? Is there a falsifiable test? Where would a smart practitioner push back?"
- "Give me a blank scaffold for a full BrainLift on [topic]."

Prompts to never send (red):
- "Write my SPOVs for [topic]." / "Draft the insights." / "Fill in the Knowledge Tree."
- "Give me a first draft of the whole BrainLift and I'll edit it."

If an AI ever hands you a finished-looking Insight or SPOV, **don't paste it in.** Read it, disagree with it, and write your own version from scratch. That act of reaction is the practice.

## One more nuance: honesty is what makes you defensible

When you (or AI) gather DOK1, be honest about weak, null, or contested evidence. It's tempting to only collect facts that support the position you want to take — but the *honesty* is exactly what makes your eventual SPOV defensible. The strongest spiky POVs are the ones staked *despite* a fairly presented evidence base, not the ones propped up by cherry-picked support.
