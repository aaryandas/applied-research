*How to tell a strong BrainLift from a weak one — and how to keep yours sharp over time.*

## The writing tests (run these before you call it done)

1. **SPOV test.** For each Spiky POV, can you name a *specific thoughtful practitioner* who would disagree on first reading? If not, it isn't spiky — sharpen the contrarian framing or demote it to an Insight.
2. **Falsifiability test.** For each SPOV, have you stated what evidence would make you retract it? If not, add the "I'd abandon this if ___" line.
3. **Cover-the-tier test.** Can each DOK4 claim be traced down through DOK3 → DOK2 → DOK1? Any claim that floats without a trail is a weak point.
4. **Scope test.** Are there sections that really belong in a *different* BrainLift? Move them. A bloated BrainLift loses its edge.
5. **Practitioner test.** Could a competent newcomer, reading only your Blueprints, execute the work? If not, they're under-specified.
6. **LLM test.** If an AI had to make a design decision using only this BrainLift, would it have enough grounding to choose well — and enough caveats to avoid overreaching?

## Common failure modes (and the fix)

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft SPOVs** | Claims everyone already agrees with | Sharpen the contrarian framing, or demote to an Insight |
| **Missing falsifiability** | Claims stated as truths, no retraction condition | Add a "what would make me wrong" line |
| **Insights that are summaries** | Restating what a source said, no original rule | Turn it into a decision rule, or move it to DOK1 |
| **Floating Blueprints** | Steps not tied to any tier below | Annotate each step with the Insight/KT it depends on |
| **Bloated Knowledge Tree** | Branches that describe the field generally | Cut any branch no SPOV/Insight/Blueprint cites |
| **Under-specified DOK1** | Titles listed, findings not extracted | Bullet the specific facts, not the abstract |
| **Scope creep** | Started about X, now also covers Y and Z | Split into separate BrainLifts + out-of-scope pointers |
| **Anonymous authorship** | No named owner | Name one |
| **AI-generated synthesis** | Long, polished, appeared in one sitting | Rewrite the DOK2/3/4 tiers yourself, from scratch |

## Working with a BrainLift over time

A BrainLift is not a one-shot document. Three things to internalize:

- **The value is in the reaction, not the construction.** It only becomes *yours* by being argued with — read each claim, compare it to what you know, push back, sharpen. A BrainLift that's never been argued with is still a draft.
- **Iteration cascades through the tiers.** They're not independent. Editing a DOK1 summary can invalidate the KT branch that cites it; editing a KT branch can invalidate an Insight; editing an Insight can invalidate a Blueprint step or an SPOV's consequences. When you change a lower layer, flag the upper layers that depend on it as *stale* and revisit them.
- **Prune more than you add.** BrainLifts get stronger through subtraction. A marginal source, a weak Insight, or a soft SPOV dilutes the rest. Ten load-bearing items beat twenty mediocre ones.
- **Come back with fresh eyes.** Build a version, iterate once or twice, then step away. Returning cold is when you catch the floating claims, the scope creep, and the SPOVs that turned out not to be what you actually believe.

## A 60-second self-review

Before you share a BrainLift, ask:

- Does the **Purpose** name who it's for and what they can decide — specifically enough to reject off-topic material?
- Are there **3–6 SPOVs**, each contrarian and each with a falsifiable test?
- Do the **Insights** read as rules you *derived*, not facts you *found*?
- Could someone **execute** the Blueprints without you in the room?
- Can you **trace** any top-level claim to a citation in under a minute?
- Does **DOK1 name its own limitations** honestly?
- Is there a **named owner**?
- Did **you** write every word of DOK2, DOK3, and DOK4?

If all eight are yes, you have a real BrainLift — not a wiki page, and not something an AI produced. That last one is the one that matters most.
