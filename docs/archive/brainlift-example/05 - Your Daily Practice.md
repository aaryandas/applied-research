*How you actually start. You don't begin by writing a full BrainLift — you begin with one short note a day.*

## The on-ramp

The full BrainLift in `04` looks intimidating because it's a destination, not a starting point. The way you get there is a **daily practice**: roughly **one hour a day** building real expertise, captured as **one short dated note**.

A daily note is *not* a finished BrainLift. It's the raw input — a few facts, your sources, and maybe one line of your own thinking — that accretes over days into a topic BrainLift. Most days you add a little. Insights and spiky POVs crystallize over weeks, not in an afternoon.

## The daily rhythm

1. **Spend ~1 hour** reading, watching, or doing something on one bounded topic.
2. **Spend ~5–15 minutes writing** it up in a dated note. That's it. Don't pad.
3. **Stay in a lane** for a stretch — daily notes on the same topic compound fast. Jumping topics every day gives you a pile of trivia; staying in a lane for two weeks gives you a BrainLift.

## Keep it short — this is the most common mistake

New people try to write a polished mini-essay every day. Don't. **Simple notes and summaries are enough.** Aim for a few DOK1 facts and, on the days you have one, a single line of your own thinking. **Blank DOK3/DOK4 sections on a given day are normal and correct** — you write an Insight or an SPOV only when you genuinely have one.

A long, polished document appearing daily is the red flag that AI wrote it (see `06`). Short and honest beats long and generic, every time.

## The daily note skeleton

File path: `Daily BrainLift/YYYY-MM-DD.md`. First line is the date as plain text (Obsidian shows the filename, so no big heading). Second line is your topic.

```
June 25, 2026
**Topic:**
**Time:** ~__ min   **Source(s):**

## DOK1 — Facts / what I took in today
(raw facts, definitions, numbers, quotes — AI may help gather these)
-

## Experts / sources worth following
- Name — why follow — where (X / blog / Substack / Scholar)

---
*Below the line is YOURS. Do not AI-generate. Leave blank on days you don't have one.*

## In my own words (DOK2 summary)
-

## DOK3 — Insight candidate (a decision rule, not a fact)
-

## DOK4 — Spiky POV candidate (a claim AI would disagree with; falsifiable)
-
```

The line `--- Below the line is YOURS` is the important divider. Everything **above** it (facts, experts) is stuff AI can help you gather. Everything **below** it is yours to write, in your own words.

## A filled-in example (what a real day looks like)

Notice: the facts above the line are dense and sourced; the part below the line is short, rough, and clearly a human thinking out loud. That's exactly right.

```
July 9, 2026
**Topic:** Spaced repetition — does the spacing interval actually matter?
**Time:** ~55 min   **Source(s):** Cepeda et al. 2006 meta-analysis; Anki manual

## DOK1 — Facts / what I took in today
- Spacing effect: information reviewed at intervals is retained far better than
  massed ("crammed") review. [research — Cepeda et al. 2006, meta-analysis of 254 studies]
- Optimal gap scales with the retention interval: to remember something for ~1 month,
  reviews spaced ~1 week apart beat same-day repetition. [research — Cepeda et al. 2008]
- The gain is large: distributed practice improved final-test recall by ~10–30 pts
  over massed in many studies. [research]
- SM-2 (the Anki/SuperMemo algorithm) just operationalizes this: each successful
  recall multiplies the next interval by an "ease factor." [practitioner — Anki manual]

## Experts / sources worth following
- Nicholas Cepeda — the canonical spacing meta-analyses — Google Scholar
- Piotr Wozniak — invented SM-2 / SuperMemo — supermemo.com

---
*Below the line is YOURS. Do not AI-generate.*

## In my own words (DOK2 summary)
- Spacing beats cramming, and the *gap* should get bigger the longer you want to
  remember something. The algorithm is just a scheduler for this.

## DOK3 — Insight candidate (a decision rule, not a fact)
- (nothing solid yet — need another day on how interval length interacts with
  difficulty before I trust a rule)

## DOK4 — Spiky POV candidate
- (blank today)
```

## How dailies become a BrainLift

- Pick a lane and stay in it. When a topic has accumulated enough DOK1 facts and experts — **and you have real DOK3/DOK4 of your own** — it's ready to graduate.
- Promote it into a proper full BrainLift in its own folder, using the structure in `02` and the template in `07`.
- **Prune as you go.** Ten load-bearing facts beat fifty. Cut what no insight uses.
- Don't rush the promotion. If all your dailies are DOK1 facts with blank thinking below the line, you're not ready — do more daily reps first. A BrainLift manufactured to hit a deadline is the failure this whole practice is designed to avoid.

## Topics don't have to be about your project

Anything you want to become the in-house expert on: AI-forward practices, go-to-market strategy, team management, learning science, developmental psychology, a technical domain. Pick something you'll still care about in two weeks.
